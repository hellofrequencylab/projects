The evidence behind [`architecture-v0.md`](architecture-v0.md). Compiled from
four parallel desk-research passes on the same day.

### ESP32 facts that drive the design (all **S**, ESP-IDF master)

- **Frame size.** ESP-NOW v1 carries 250 B per frame; v2 carries **1470 B**.
  A v2 node receives both versions. A v1 node truncates or drops anything
  over 250 B.
- **Peers.** The limit is 20, of which 17 can be encrypted. Broadcast and
  unencrypted unicast frames need no peer entry.
- **Shared channel.** ESP-NOW and a SoftAP must share one channel. If the
  station side is connected to an upstream AP, that AP's channel wins.
- **LR beacons.** An LR-only AP beacons in LR mode, so normal phones can't see
  it.
- **ESP32-C5 at 5 GHz.** ESP-NOW works on 5 GHz channels 36–177, but LR is
  2.4 GHz only. No 5 GHz measurements were found.

### Reference meshes

- **Meshtastic.**
  - Frames are 256 B with ~237 B of payload.
  - Routing is managed flooding with a default hop limit of 3 (max 7).
  - Meshes of 60+ nodes congest on LongFast.
  - **The killer is periodic NodeInfo, telemetry and position beaconing, not
    user data.** **S**[meshtastic blog, mspmesh]
- **MeshCore.**
  - Frames are 255 B with 184 B of payload.
  - The first contact floods; after that, traffic is source-routed using
    1-byte repeater IDs, up to 64 hops.
  - Leaf nodes don't relay. **S**[MeshCore.h, eastmesh wiki]
- **Bitchat.**
  - BLE mesh with a TTL of 7, adaptively clamped to 5.
  - Dedup uses a 1000-entry LRU.
  - Fragments are ~469 B.
  - Falls back to Nostr when online.
  - Saw real use in Nepal (2025) and Iran (Jan 2026). **S**[whitepaper, press]

## 2. Protocols and building blocks

- **Reticulum (RNS).**
  - Wire format and limits: 500 B MTU, 128-bit hash addresses, 167 B
    announces capped at 2% of airtime, 297 B link setup, works down to ~5 bps.
  - Crypto: Ed25519, X25519 and AES-CBC+HMAC, with **no algorithm agility**.
  - **License: RNS moved from MIT to a custom "Reticulum License" with a
    field-of-use clause. That is not OSI- or DFSG-compatible.**
    **S**[license.rst mirror]
  - microReticulum (C++, Apache-2.0) runs on an ESP32-S3 with PSRAM, but
    support is partial: no full Links or Resources.
  - **Implication:** borrow the ideas, don't build on the code. This
    overturns the handoff's "build on Reticulum" idea.
- **DTN Bundle Protocol v7** (RFC 9171/9172).
  - The architecture is right: store-carry-forward, lifetimes, and
    bearer-agnostic convergence layers.
  - The headers are too heavy for 250 B frames.
  - **Use:** a gateway for interop, not the on-air format.
- **Range-based set reconciliation.**
  - Negentropy (NIP-77) sorts items by (time, id) and fingerprints ranges by
    summing hashes. It is deployed in production in strfry.
  - Willow generalizes the idea to 3D ranges with capabilities (Meadowcap).
  - RAM per round is bounded, so it suits MCUs.
  - **This is the strongest reusable piece.** **S**
- **BLAKE3 + Bao.**
  - A tree hash, so any chunk can be verified against the root no matter
    where it came from.
  - Transfers can resume and draw from multiple sources.
  - **S**[bao spec]
- **Avoid Secure Scuttlebutt's model.** Its per-author hash-chained logs
  fork, partition and grow forever. Sync sets of independent signed objects
  instead. **S**
- **Routing.** Nothing scales globally on tiny nodes by itself.
  - B.A.T.M.A.N. and Babel work for islands only.
  - Yggdrasil's tree routing breaks under partition.
  - Geographic routing has O(neighbours) state.
  - DTN Spray-and-Wait bounds the number of copies.
  - **S**[various]
- **Speed multipliers.**
  - Fountain codes: RaptorQ (RFC 6330) is near-optimal but
    **Qualcomm-IPR-encumbered**. LT/online codes are free. RLNC lets relays
    recode, but **patent risk is unverified**.
  - Compression: heatshrink or Tamp with tiny RAM, plus static dictionaries.
  - Header compression: a SCHC-style (RFC 8724) context ID.
- **Crypto on ESP32.**
  - Ed25519 verify takes ~28 ms in software.
  - mbedTLS's 25519 is slow (~100 ms), so use Monocypher or a tuned library.
  - AES and SHA-256 are in hardware (SHA-256 ~28 MB/s).
  - **S**[esp32.com, eprint 2021/471]
- **Post-quantum sizes** (FIPS 203/204/205):

  | Algorithm | Public key | Signature / ciphertext |
  |---|---|---|
  | ML-KEM-768 | 1184 B | 1088 B ciphertext |
  | ML-DSA-44 | 1312 B | 2420 B signature |
  | SLH-DSA-128s | 32 B | 7856 B signature |

  - NIST draft IR 8547: ECC deprecated after 2030, disallowed after 2035.
  - Speed on MCUs is acceptable; **size is the problem.** **S**

## 3. Infrastructure to ride (bearers beyond our own radios)

| Bearer | Rate | Survives grid-down? | Legal constraint that shapes the protocol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Internet (TCP/QUIC, Nostr-style relays) | Mbps–Gbps | No | None |
| HF ham: JS8Call / FT8 / ARDOP / VARA | 5 bps – ~7 kbps | **Yes** (batteries) | **No encryption on ham bands** (§97.113). Signing is widely considered OK, but the FCC hasn't ruled on it. Prefer the open modems; VARA is closed. HF symbol-rate limit removed 2024-01-08 **S** |
| VHF/UHF ham: APRS, M17, NPR, VARA FM | 1.2 – ~25 kbps (NPR up to 500 kbps outside the US) | Yes | US 2 m / 70 cm **symbol-rate limits still in force**, as of the eCFR checked 9/2026 **S** |
| AREDN (802.11 on ham microwave) | 10–100+ Mbps | If towers have power | No encryption |
| Iridium SBD / Certus | 340 B messages / 22–88 kbps | Yes (independent) | Commercial, metered |
| Starlink / direct-to-cell / NB-NTN | 50–300 Mbps / SMS-class | Partly | **Vendor and state kill switches are real**: Myanmar cutoffs, the Ukraine whitelist (2026), Iran dish seizures **S** |
| Broadcast datacast (ATSC 3.0, DVB, DRM) | Mbps, one-way | If the station has a generator | Needs a broadcaster partner. New Mexico PBS did wildfire datacasts **S** |
| Powerline (HomePlug/G.hn) | 20–200 Mbps | **No**, it dies with the grid | Same transformer only |
| Your own copper (SHDSL/VDSL back-to-back), fiber, 10BASE-T1L | 5 Mbps @ 3 km / Gbps / 10 Mbps @ 1 km | Yes, with power at both ends | Only on wire you own or may use |
| Sneakernet (SD/USB) | TB per trip | **Always** | None. Cuba's El Paquete is the precedent |

**Dead services, don't design for them:** Swarm (shut down 2025-03), Othernet
(out of business 2025-11). **S**

**Lessons from real emergencies.**

- **Puerto Rico after Maria:** low-rate HF email was enough. The bottleneck
  was trained people and message formats.
- **Gaza:** donated eSIMs kept people connected.
- **Iran 2026:** Bitchat spread mostly through sideloading.
- **Overall:** usability and procedure beat bandwidth. **S**

## 4. Regulatory hazards (check before choosing default radio settings)

- **US 902–928 MHz.**
  - Hackaday (2026-09-17) reports that Meshtastic's and MeshCore's default
    250 kHz LoRa presets may violate §15.247, which requires ≥500 kHz
    bandwidth for non-hopping digital modulation. Whether §15.247 applies
    at all is disputed.
  - Meshtastic alpha has already changed its defaults.
  - NextNav's petition to take over parts of the band is still pending.
  - **Default to ≥500 kHz BW or real frequency hopping in the US.** **S**
- **EU 868 MHz:** mostly a 1% duty cycle. The 869.4–869.65 MHz sub-band
  allows 10% at 27 dBm. **S/E**
- **EU 2.4 GHz** (EN 300 328): 20 dBm EIRP. Above 10 dBm you need adaptivity
  (listen-before-talk) or a 10% medium-utilization cap. **S**
- **Ham bands:** cleartext plus signature only, and no business traffic. The
  router must enforce this per interface; it can't be left to users.

## 5. Forward look (5–15 years)

- **Wi-Fi end:** cost per Mbps keeps falling roughly 10x every 5 years.
  - ESP32-C5/C61 bring Wi-Fi 6.
  - The ESP32-E22 co-processor brings Wi-Fi 6E with 2x2 MIMO.
  - **E**/**S**
- **Long-range sub-GHz:** limited by physics and regulation, not silicon.
  It will stay at kbps.
  - **Design consequence:** plan for a ~10⁸ spread in link rates, from 5 bps
    HF to Gbps fiber.
- **Cheap SDR:** no real full-duplex SDR exists at $20–60 today. Pluto is
  €180+; CaribouLite is $69+.
  - A software-defined CyberMesh PHY is a post-2030 optional tier, and
    certification, not silicon, is the blocker.
  - openwifi on Pluto reaches ~50 Mbps. **S**
- **Direct-to-device satellite** will reach cheap boards only as
  carrier-gated, metered NB-NTN uplinks. Lacuna's LoRa-to-satellite service
  has a free developer tier. **S**
- **Data mules:**
  - One 256 GB card on a daily bus averages ~24 Mbps.
  - The real limit is contact-time transfer rate: a 10–60 s Wi-Fi pass moves
    0.06–4 GB.
  - Coding over chunks gave a 54% gain in a Toulouse transit study. **S/E**
- **Post-quantum:**
  - Plan crypto agility now.
  - Hash-based signatures (SLH-DSA, NIST SP 800-230 draft) suit long-lived
    archives.
  - Stateful LMS/XMSS is unsafe on nodes that can lose state. **S**
